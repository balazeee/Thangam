The Life and Legacy of T. Kaliyaperumal Chettiar

From bullock presses to a golden legacy—a journey of honesty, grit, and unwavering purity.

A Visionary's Evolution

Mr. Kaliyaperumal Chettiar stands as an industry veteran whose life's work seamlessly bridges the gap between heritage and modern innovation. Starting his journey with the traditional Maatu Chekku (bullock-driven oil press), he witnessed and drove the evolution toward modern extraction systems. Yet, amidst technological advancements, his core commitment remained unchanged: preserving the traditional quality, aroma, and purity of the oil that his ancestors perfected.

🌾 The Early Years (1962)

Rooted in a long lineage of oil merchants from Andipalayam, a village near Cuddalore, They traditionally produced sesame oil using bullock-drawn presses and sold it to surrounding villages. He completed his S.S.L.C. in 1961. However, driven by an entrepreneurial spirit, he ventured out on his own at the tender age of 18. His early days were defined by hard labor—purchasing oil seeds directly from local markets, meticulously overseeing the pressing process, and personally delivering the fresh oil to nearby shops. This hands-on experience laid the bedrock of his profound knowledge of oil quality.

✨

Guiding Philosophy

"Honesty, integrity, and hard work with faith will surely bring success."

🛣️ Journey to Puducherry (1975)

The First Attempt (1968): Because many of his relatives lived in Puducherry, he frequently visited the city and wanted to start a mill there. In 1968, he rented a building on Vazhudavur Road. However, the building lacked an electricity connection, forcing him to return to his village and resume traditional business.
The Breakthrough (1975): His desire never faded. On July 10, 1975, he purchased a mill building behind Ananda Theatre in Thiruvalluvar Nagar, Puducherry. He installed four wooden presses (PINDO) powered by electricity and began production, marking a pivotal moment in the brand's history.

🏷️ Building the 'Thangam' Brand

Recognizing market shifts and health trends, he strategically transitioned the focus from groundnut oil to premium sesame oil. In a poignant tribute to his father, Mr. Thangaraj Chettiar, he christened the brand 'Thangam', meaning 'Gold' in Tamil—a name that perfectly encapsulated the purity and premium quality of their offering.

Expansion: By 1977, he moved from wooden presses to Mini Expellers to process groundnut (peanut) oil. Eventually, he replaced those with two Standard Expellers to scale up production, selling groundnut oil as far as Mumbai.
Sesame Focus: Despite the success in groundnuts, he returned his primary focus to sesame oil, citing it as his "traditional family business."
Marketing Advice: He admits that early retail marketing was difficult. He once spent ₹3,000 on travel to an area only to sell three boxes of oil. However, he persisted, believing that if the quality is good, the brand will eventually sell itself.

📈 Market Evolution

Government Regulations: At one point, the Central Government mandated that Vanaspati (vegetable ghee) must contain 5% sesame oil. This created a massive surge in demand. His mill supplied large quantities to major companies like Hindustan Lever, Tungabhadra Oil Mill, and Wipro Products.

Modern Branding: When the government later relaxed the Vanaspati rules, the bulk market slowed down. Chettiar adapted by shifting to consumer pouch packing and retail sales. He credits the Puducherry government for providing subsidies (50% for packing and film machines) and tax exemptions that helped him transition.
